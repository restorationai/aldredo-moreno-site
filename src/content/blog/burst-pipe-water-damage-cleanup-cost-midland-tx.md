**TL;DR:** Burst pipe water damage cleanup in Midland, TX typically costs $1,500 to $3,500 for a single room with a fast response, $3,500 to $7,500 for multiple rooms needing drywall removal, and $7,500 to $15,000 or more for a whole-floor loss with extensive flood cuts. The biggest cost drivers are how long the water sat before cleanup started, how much drywall and flooring has to come out, and how many days of air movers and dehumidifiers the drying takes. Pipe repair itself is billed separately by a licensed plumber.

If a supply line let go overnight in your Midland home and you're standing in a wet hallway wondering what this is going to cost before you even call anyone, here's the honest range. A clean water loss, the most common kind when a supply pipe or fitting fails, is far cheaper to resolve than one that sat for days or involved sewage. The numbers below reflect typical industry ranges for this kind of job in West Texas, not a quote for your specific property.

## How Much Does a Burst Pipe Cleanup Cost in Midland, TX?

Most Midland homeowners pay somewhere between $1,500 and $15,000 for burst pipe water damage cleanup, depending almost entirely on how much area got wet and how long the water was standing before extraction started.

| Scope of the job | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean supply water, caught within 24 hours | $1,500 - $3,500 |
| Two to three rooms, drywall flood cuts and baseboard removal | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Whole floor, extensive flood cuts, pad and flooring removal | $7,500 - $15,000+ |
| Water sat 48+ hours before cleanup (contents pack-out, added drying) | add $1,500 - $4,000+ |
| Frozen pipe burst requiring wall or slab access to reach the break | $4,000 - $10,000+ |

These ranges cover extraction, drying equipment, and removal of affected porous materials like wet drywall, baseboard, and carpet pad. They do not include the plumber's charge to actually repair the broken pipe or fitting; that's a separate trade and a separate invoice.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down?

The three biggest variables are the water's condition, how much material has to be cut out, and the square footage affected.

A burst supply line almost always starts as what the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) calls Category 1 water, meaning it came from a sanitary source. That's the cheapest water to clean up. But Category 1 water doesn't stay clean if it sits against drywall, carpet pad, or baseboard for more than a day or two; the longer it sits, the more likely it's treated as a more contaminated category, which usually means more material comes out instead of being dried in place.

Drywall flood cuts (removing the wet section of wall, typically 12 to 24 inches above the visible waterline) are priced by linear foot and add up fast on an open floor plan. Baseboard and carpet pad removal are usually cheaper line items but still factor into the total. A pipe that broke behind a kitchen cabinet or inside a slab penetration also costs more to access than one that let go under an open sink.

## How Many Days of Drying Equipment Does the Job Need?

A typical burst pipe drying job runs three to five days of air movers and a dehumidifier running continuously, and West Texas's naturally dry air actually works in your favor here.

Midland's low ambient humidity means a dehumidifier doesn't have to fight the outside air the way it would in a coastal city, so drying often finishes closer to the three-day mark than the five. Equipment is billed per unit per day: expect several axial air movers positioned around the wet perimeter plus one low-grain refrigerant dehumidifier for a typical room-scale loss. A technician checks moisture content in the affected studs and subfloor daily with a meter and only pulls the equipment once readings return to a dry standard, not on a fixed schedule.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover a Burst Pipe?

Most standard homeowners policies cover a burst pipe because it's considered sudden and accidental damage, not the result of neglect.

The distinction insurers draw is between a pipe that failed suddenly (a hard freeze, a defective fitting, water hammer) and a slow leak that went unaddressed for months, which is typically excluded as gradual damage or wear and tear. The [Insurance Information Institute explains what's generally covered under a standard homeowners policy](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) and the pattern holds in Texas too. Document the damage with photos before cleanup starts, keep any receipts, and start mitigation promptly: most policies require you to limit further damage, and an adjuster will expect to see that effort reflected in the timeline. We've covered the broader coverage picture in more detail in [our guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

## Why Do Frozen Pipe Bursts Cost More?

A frozen pipe burst usually costs more because the break is often hidden inside an exterior wall, an attic run, or a slab penetration, which takes extra labor to access before cleanup can even start.

Midland doesn't freeze often, but when a hard freeze does hit, like the February 2021 winter storm that burst pipes across West Texas for days, the damage tends to be worse because multiple fixtures fail at once and homeowners are often without power to run drying equipment right away. If you're thinking ahead of the next hard freeze, our [burst pipe emergency checklist](/blog/burst-pipe-emergency-checklist/) covers the steps that limit damage in the first hour.

## Why Does Waiting Past 24 to 48 Hours Raise the Final Bill?

Waiting past 24 to 48 hours to start cleanup raises the cost because wet materials begin supporting unwanted growth in that window, which usually means more demolition instead of drying.

The [EPA's guidance on moisture and indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) notes that wet building materials can start growing unwanted organisms within one to two days. Once that happens, drywall and insulation that could have been dried and saved typically get cut out and replaced instead, which adds material and labor cost on top of the original drying job. Acting fast isn't just about comfort; it's the single biggest lever on the final number.
