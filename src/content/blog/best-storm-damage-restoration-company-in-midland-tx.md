## What Should You Look For in a Storm Damage Restoration Company?

Look for a company that answers the phone when the storm is actually happening, has a documented track record (not just a high average on a thin number of reviews), and is upfront about what it does and doesn't handle. Those three filters eliminate most of the guesswork.

Ask directly whether the company handles water extraction and structural drying, or whether it's primarily a mold, roofing, or general-contracting outfit that picked up a water-damage category on its Google listing. Ask how fast someone can be on-site, since standing water and wet drywall start supporting mold growth within 24 to 48 hours according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold). And ask whether the company documents moisture readings and photos as it works, since that documentation is what your insurance adjuster will want to see. Our [storm damage insurance claim checklist](/blog/storm-damage-insurance-claim-checklist/) walks through exactly what to gather before you call your insurer.

## Who Handles Hail and Wind Damage Inside the House? (And Who Handles the Roof?)

A storm damage restoration company like ACS Enterprise handles everything water-related once it's inside your home, while a licensed roofing contractor handles the shingles, decking, and flashing that let the water in. These are two different trades with two different scopes of work, and conflating them is the most common mistake homeowners make after a hailstorm.

In practice, the sequence usually runs: a roofer or restoration crew tarps the breach to stop further intrusion, a water damage crew extracts standing water and sets up drying equipment, and a roofer separately schedules the permanent repair. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/replacing-your-roof.html) publishes guidance on roof claims specifically because homeowners often don't realize these are separate line items on a claim. If you're not sure which call to make first, our guide on [choosing a restoration company](/blog/choosing-a-restoration-company/) breaks down how to vet both trades before a storm, not during one.

If water is actively coming through your ceiling right now, the interior call comes first. Standing water doesn't wait for a roofing estimate.

Midland's flat terrain and caliche soil mean storm water has nowhere to drain quickly, which is why interior flooding after a hail or wind event often outlasts the storm itself by hours or days. A fast, documented response to the water matters as much as the roof repair that follows it.
