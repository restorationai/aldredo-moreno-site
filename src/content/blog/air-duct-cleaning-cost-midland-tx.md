Homes with two HVAC systems, hard-to-reach ductwork, or sanitizing add-ons usually run $600 to $1,200. Price is driven by vent count, system count, and how much labor it takes to get equipment to the ducts, not a flat per-room fee.

If you're getting quotes that swing from $99 to $900 for what sounds like the same job, you're not imagining things. A lot of West Texas homes collect visible dust and fine sand inside the ductwork between dust storms, and the pricing on this service varies more than almost any other cleaning service homeowners shop for. Here's what a real whole-house job actually runs, and why the $99 ad you saw isn't the number you'll pay.

## What does air duct cleaning cost in Midland, TX?

Most single-story Midland homes with one HVAC system pay $300 to $600 for a full duct cleaning, including the supply and return runs, registers, and the main trunk line. Homes with two systems, a second story, or an attic that's hard to crawl through typically land between $600 and $1,200.

The number moves based on four things: how many supply and return vents the home has, how many HVAC systems feed those vents, how the crew has to access the ductwork (attic crawl versus a slab home with ducts in a soffit or closet), and whether you add sanitizing or a dryer vent cleaning to the visit. A 1,500 square foot home with 10 to 12 vents and one system is the cheapest version of this job. A 3,200 square foot home with two systems and 20+ vents, common in the newer Midland and Odessa subdivisions, costs more because it's genuinely more ductwork and more labor hours.

## Why do some ads say $99 for whole-house duct cleaning?

The $99 or "per vent" ads you see are almost always a bait price for a much smaller job than the one you actually need. That number usually covers a limited number of vents, excludes the main trunk lines and returns, and turns into a much larger invoice once the technician is in your attic pointing out everything the base price didn't include.

Watch for three things on any quote: whether the price is per-system or per-vent, whether it includes the main supply and return trunks (not just the register covers), and whether sanitizing is bundled in or an upsell once the crew arrives. A legitimate whole-house quote should name your system count and approximate vent count before anyone shows up, not after.

Per-vent pricing can make sense for a small job, like cleaning three or four vents in one renovated room. For an actual whole-house cleaning, a flat whole-system quote based on a walkthrough or a few photos of your attic and supply registers is the honest version of this service.

## Is air duct cleaning worth it in a dusty West Texas home?

Air duct cleaning is worth it when ducts show visible dust buildup, debris, or pest activity, not as a routine annual service. The [EPA's indoor air quality guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) is clear that duct cleaning hasn't been shown to actually improve air quality on a fixed schedule, and there's no evidence it prevents health problems in a typical home.

What changes that answer in Midland and the Permian Basin: a home after a blowing dust event, a recent renovation that left drywall dust in the system, visible rodent or insect activity in the ductwork, or registers that blow out a visible cloud of dust when the system kicks on. Those are concrete signs the ductwork needs attention, not a calendar reminder.

If you just want a baseline, pull a supply register cover off in a rarely-used room and look inside with a flashlight. Visible gray buildup along the duct walls, not just a thin film on the register itself, is the signal worth acting on.

## What else adds to the price beyond the basics?

A few add-ons show up on most Midland quotes, and each one has a reason behind it.

- **Multiple HVAC systems**: each system is a separate set of trunk lines, so a two-system home roughly doubles the labor, not the price, since mobilization is shared.
- **Attic access difficulty**: a cramped attic with low clearance or blown insulation takes longer to work in safely than a walk-up attic.
- **Sanitizing treatment**: an antimicrobial fogging step after the physical cleaning, priced separately because it's optional and not every home needs it.
- **Dryer vent cleaning**: a different duct entirely, but often bundled in the same visit since the equipment and crew are already on site.
- **Commercial buildings**: larger duct runs and commercial-grade equipment push pricing into a custom quote rather than a flat residential range.

## How often should Midland homeowners actually clean their ducts?

There's no fixed annual schedule, not even EPA guidance supports one, so the honest answer is: when the signs above show up, not on a set calendar. Most Midland homes that aren't dealing with dust intrusion, pests, or a recent renovation can go several years between cleanings.

If you want a second opinion before paying for a job you might not need, ask whoever quotes you to show you the inside of a duct run on camera before and after, not just tell you it was dirty. The [National Air Duct Cleaners Association](https://nadca.com/) publishes general standards for what a real cleaning should include, and it's a reasonable benchmark to compare any quote against, even from a company that isn't NADCA-member certified.
