## What's the difference between hot water extraction, low-moisture cleaning, and rotary bonnet cleaning?

Hot water extraction, often called steam cleaning, injects hot water and cleaning solution deep into the carpet pile, then extracts it with a wet vacuum, pulling out the most soil but leaving carpet damp for several hours. Low-moisture cleaning uses an encapsulating solution that crystallizes dirt for vacuum pickup, with almost no drying wait. Rotary bonnet cleaning spins an absorbent pad across the surface, lifting surface soil fast but reaching less deep into the pile.

Hot water extraction is the method most carpet manufacturers recommend, and the one most carpet warranties require on a schedule, because it reaches soil trapped at the base of the fiber, not just the surface. It's the right call for carpet that hasn't been deep-cleaned in a year or more, or that's carrying oil-field dust ground into the pile. Low-moisture and bonnet methods suit light maintenance cleaning in commercial spaces that can't tolerate a long drying window, like a leasing office that needs the carpet usable within the hour.

The [IICRC](https://www.iicrc.org/), the industry's standards body for cleaning and restoration, publishes the technical guidance most legitimate carpet cleaners train against. Ask which method a company plans to use and why. A company that can explain the tradeoff knows the craft; one that just says "we steam clean everything" might not.

## What should you ask before you book?

Ask how pricing is calculated (per room, per square foot, or a flat visit fee), whether pretreatment for stains and traffic lanes is included or billed separately, who moves furniture, and whether there's a straight answer on specific stains like pet urine before the crew arrives.

- **How is price calculated?** A per-room or per-square-foot rate is easier to compare across companies than a vague "whole house" quote with no basis explained.
- **Is furniture moving included?** Some companies move light furniture at no charge and ask you to clear small items; find out before the crew shows up, not while they're standing in your living room.
- **What's the honest answer on pet urine?** Urine that's soaked into the padding or subfloor, especially older stains, can leave a faint odor or discoloration even after cleaning because the contamination sits below where the wand reaches.
