## What does a real air duct cleaning job in Midland actually include?

A complete air duct cleaning covers every supply run, every return run, and the blower compartment and evaporator coil, agitated loose and pulled into a HEPA-filtered vacuum under negative air pressure so the dust doesn't just blow back into your living room.

The technician should cut or open service access points along the main trunk lines, run a rotary brush or compressed-air whip through each branch, and keep the whole system under vacuum the entire time. That last part matters: without negative air pressure, agitating a dusty duct just redistributes the dust through the house instead of removing it.

A company that does this right will show you before-and-after photos, either on a tablet on site or sent after the job, of what came out of your registers and trunk line. If a company can't produce that proof, ask why.

## What does the NADCA ACR standard mean, and do you need a certified company?

The [National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA)](https://nadca.com/) publishes the ACR standard, short for Assessment, Cleaning, Restoration, which is the industry's written benchmark for how a duct system should be inspected, cleaned, and documented from start to finish.

ACS Enterprise is not a NADCA-certified firm, and we're not going to pretend otherwise. What the ACR standard is useful for is giving you a yardstick to hand any company you're vetting, certified or not: ask them to walk through how they assess the system before quoting, what method they use to clean it, and how they document the finished job. A company that can answer those three questions in detail is operating at the standard NADCA describes, whether or not the certification is framed on their wall. The [EPA's guidance on indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) is a useful second reference if you want to understand what duct cleaning can and can't do for the air in your home.

## How do you spot the $99 whole-house duct cleaning scam?

A legitimate whole-system clean on an average Midland home takes real labor, usually two to three hours on site, so a flat $99 price almost always means the real cost shows up once the crew is already in your house.

Watch for these patterns:

- **The quote never mentions the blower or return lines**, only "vents," which usually means just the visible supply registers get touched.
- **No equipment comes off the truck.** A real job needs a portable HEPA vacuum unit and brushes or air-whip tools, not a shop-vac and a rag.
- **An on-the-spot upsell for sanitizing or "mold treatment"** that wasn't part of the original quote, pitched as urgent once they're already inside.
- **No before-and-after photos and no written scope.** If you can't see what was actually cleaned, you have no way to know the job was done.
- **No local address, no license or insurance info when asked.** A company that won't answer basic questions over the phone isn't one you want in your attic.

## Should you hire an HVAC contractor or a restoration company for duct cleaning?

Either type of company can do the mechanical cleaning, but the difference shows up when the dirty ducts are a symptom of something bigger than ordinary dust, like a slow roof leak dripping into a return plenum or lingering smoke odor after a kitchen fire.

An HVAC contractor is the right call for routine maintenance and system tune-ups alongside the cleaning. A restoration company that also handles [water damage restoration](/services/water-damage-restoration/) and moisture issues is the better call when you suspect the ductwork got dirty, musty, or stained because of a leak, not just West Texas dust. That's the gap a company like ACS Enterprise fills: cleaning the system and knowing what to do if the crew finds evidence the mess came from water, not weather.

## Why do West Texas dust storms load up Midland ductwork faster than other regions?

Midland sits in blowing caliche and Permian Basin oilfield dust country, so homes here accumulate duct debris faster than homes in wetter climates, which means the 3- to 5-year cleaning interval recommended nationally often needs to be shorter here.

Homes along the I-20 corridor and in neighborhoods near Hogan Park take it especially hard during spring dust events, when fine caliche grit gets pulled in through returns near garage doors and exterior walls faster than the filter can catch it. If you're running your AC or heat and noticing dust settle on furniture within days of cleaning, that's often a sign the return side of the system, not just the registers, needs attention.
