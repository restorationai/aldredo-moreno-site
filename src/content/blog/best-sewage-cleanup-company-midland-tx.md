## Should You Call a Plumber or a Sewage Cleanup Company First?

Call a plumber first only if sewage is actively flowing and the source hasn't been stopped; call a restoration company once the flow is contained, or immediately if standing water has already reached flooring, drywall, or belongings. The two jobs are different trades solving different problems.

A plumber's job is the mechanical fix: clearing the clog, repairing the broken line, or replacing the failed component that caused the backup. Most plumbers are not equipped to perform Category 3 water extraction, antimicrobial application, or the structural drying and moisture documentation an insurance claim requires.

In Midland, many sewage backups come from tree root intrusion in older clay sewer laterals, a collapsed line, or a municipal sewer surcharge during heavy rain. If the backup is from a private line on your property, you'll likely need a plumber for the repair and a restoration company like ACS Enterprise for the cleanup, often on the same day. If the backup originated from the city main, the City of Midland's utilities department is the right first call for the source, with the restoration side of the cleanup running in parallel.

## What the S500 Category 3 Standard Means for Your Home

Category 3 water, also called black water, is contaminated water that contains sewage, chemicals, or other pathogens, and it's the classification every raw sewage backup falls into under the [ANSI/S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). That classification changes how the job has to be done, not just how fast.

Under S500 guidance, Category 3 water calls for containment of the affected area, PPE for anyone working in it, removal of porous materials that absorbed contaminated water (carpet pad, some drywall, insulation), and antimicrobial treatment of surfaces that can be cleaned and saved. The [EPA's guidance on cleaning up after flooding](https://www.epa.gov/flooded-homes) reinforces the same baseline: contaminated water and the materials it touches need to be handled differently than a clean-water leak, both for health and for how the repair holds up over time.

This is also the paperwork that protects your insurance claim. Photo documentation of the contamination level, moisture readings before and after drying, and a written scope of what was removed versus treated are what separate a smooth claim from a denied one. [Texas homeowners' policies vary on what sewage backup coverage looks like](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/when-are-water-damage-and-mold-covered-by-insurance.html), and many require a specific sewer backup endorsement, so ask your adjuster this question before cleanup starts if you have time.
