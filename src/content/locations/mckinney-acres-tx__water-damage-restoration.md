## Why McKinney Acres Properties See Water Damage

McKinney Acres sits on the kind of expansive clay and caliche soil common across Midland County, and that soil shifts hard with every wet-dry cycle. Foundations here move more than homeowners realize, and that movement stresses the plumbing runs underneath slab-on-grade homes, which make up a large share of the housing stock in this part of the county. A hairline crack in a slab pipe can leak for weeks before it ever shows up as a stain on the carpet, which means by the time it's visible, water has often already wicked into the pad, the baseboards, and sometimes the wall cavity. Add in the area's reliance on private wells and older pressure tanks on some properties, and a failed fitting can dump a surprising volume of water before anyone notices.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in McKinney Acres

The first step on any call is finding where the water actually is, not just where it's visible. On slab homes we check moisture readings along the full perimeter of the affected room, because water under a slab leak travels along the path of least resistance and can surface ten feet from the actual break. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring and, where needed, from under cabinet toe-kicks and baseboards. Structural drying follows, set up and monitored against the IICRC S500 standard for water damage, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed and adjusted based on daily moisture readings rather than left running on a fixed schedule. On homes with original 1970s and 1980s plumbing, which is common in this area, we also flag any fittings that look like repeat-failure risks so the homeowner can address them before the next event.
